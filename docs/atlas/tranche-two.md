# Second collection · water, knowledge and collective life

The collection adds 20 moments and five journeys without changing the original period datasets, the life-expectancy snapshot or the fingerprint component. The new source records are in `data/atlas/tranche-two.json`; each has a source link, an original concise account, and a specific limit to the comparison. They appear in map search, the date slider, related moments, stories and saved collections automatically.

## Editorial choices

- **Water and food:** Kuk, Budj Bim, Dujiangyan, Shushtar and Rani-ki-Vav extend the earlier Angkor and city histories. They make local knowledge, maintenance and collective infrastructure visible instead of implying that innovation happens only in industrial Europe.
- **Knowledge and access:** Nalanda, Timbuktu, quipus, Braille and the transistor broaden the existing writing-to-Web theme. Hōkūleʻa adds sophisticated embodied navigation and cultural renewal, avoiding the equation of knowledge with written documents alone.
- **Collective action:** Seneca Falls, Montgomery, the Green Belt Movement and Liberia's women's peace movement extend the existing political-rights stories with organisation, practical participation, and differences in goals.
- **Networks and cooperation:** Andean roads, the Postal Union, the Metre Convention and the Montreal Protocol introduce the often-invisible rules and infrastructure behind shared systems.

## Dating and interpretation

Kuk's 4500 BCE anchor represents evidence of developed agriculture approximately 6,500 years ago; it is not a founding date. Budj Bim's 4000 BCE anchor represents the aquaculture tradition's approximate six-millennia timescale, not the much longer Gunditjmara relationship with Country. Both use approximate dates and state the distinction in the story.

Shushtar is anchored around 250 CE, following UNESCO's description of the main third-century system, with older foundations clearly distinguished. Nalanda uses a fifth-century institutional anchor, not the age of its earliest archaeological remains. Rani-ki-Vav and Dujiangyan use century-level approximations. Timbuktu, quipus and Andean roads represent periods of use or flourishing, not claims to an exact invention year. Braille uses an approximate 1825 anchor for development during the 1820s.

John Snow's story avoids the familiar claim that one map or removing one pump handle instantly solved cholera. The source review and the account distinguish a declining outbreak from the broader evidence for waterborne transmission. Hōkūleʻa names Mau Piailug's Micronesian tradition and anchors the map at the Tahitian arrival. Movement accounts credit organisation and wider processes rather than attributing outcomes to a lone hero.

## Sources

Institutional sources include UNESCO's World Heritage descriptions, Museo Larco's quipu interpretation, RNIB's historical collection, the Polynesian Voyaging Society's voyage account, the US National Park Service, Nobel Prize biographies and scientific background, the Universal Postal Union, BIPM, and UNEP's Ozone Secretariat. The Snow record cites a scholarly historical review. Source URLs are attached to each moment and available from its story. HTTP reachability and historical verification are separate: `source-audit.json` records automated responses, including access protection and rate limits.
