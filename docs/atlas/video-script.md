# History Loom — directed film

Status: recorded production script. Voice and delivery approved from the 28-second audition. 1920 × 1080, 30 fps. Runtime approximately 2 minutes 33 seconds. Exact narration cues and chapter times are saved in the delivered edit-timeline.json.

The film asks what connects distant eras, reveals how comparisons are made, and distinguishes editorial interpretation from measured observations. No claim that the dataset proves a universal historical cycle.

## 01 · An invitation

Picture: open on the illustrated Explore hero. Hold for two seconds before the first sentence. A slow scroll reveals the map. Keep cursor still until it has a purpose.

Narration:

> What makes two distant moments in history feel familiar? A new invention. A struggle for a voice. The fear that the old rules no longer work. History Loom follows those connections, across centuries and around the world.

Cue: reveal map on “around the world”. Sound: barely audible air and a warm, sustained texture.

## 02 · One moment opens a world

Picture: select One block. A thousand voices. (the Diamond Sutra, 868) on the live map. Open its story, point toward a related moment, then expand the evidence and British Library source link. The narration deliberately avoids inventing a causal relationship between moments.

Narration:

> Start with a moment. Open its story. What changed? Who gained something? Who was left out? Each connection offers another way into the past. You can follow the thread, or stop and explore the sources behind it.

Cue: story opens on “Open its story”; sources visible on “sources”. Keep text panels legible and give each transition breathing room.

## 03 · Follow a thread

Picture: The first viral ideas journey. Move from the Diamond Sutra through Gutenberg to the Web, then choose They made ideas easier to copy and hold on the explanation, including what differs between the moments.

Narration:

> A journey carries a question through different places and different lives. Watch how people share knowledge, survive upheaval, or challenge power. The connections invite comparison. The details remind us how much has changed.

Cue: second stop on “different places”. Restrained soft sweep at the transition; no literal battle or crowd sounds.

## 04 · Familiar pressures

Picture: Pattern lab → History’s fingerprints. Show the default French era and its closest cross-country comparison. Frame the six-axis shapes and both era names. Hold before changing the era once. The existing page remains unmodified.

Narration:

> Now look beneath the story. Compare an era through six pressures: wealth, livelihoods, technology, trust, hope, and military pressure. History Loom finds another era with a similar shape. These scores are historical interpretations. They help us ask sharper questions about resemblance, rather than treating two societies as the same.

Cue: full fingerprint visible before “six pressures”. The interpretation badge stays visible. Let the shapes settle before the next sentence.

## 05 · Put the dates back

Picture: Echoes across time → Group by similarity. Select a point, inspect its neighbours, then click Place in time. After the intervals are visible, enable the eighty-year guide.

Narration:

> Step back, and whole groups come into view. Here, eras gather by similar pressures, without using their dates to decide where they belong. Now put those dates back. The gaps vary. An eighty-year guide gives us something to compare against, but these examples do not establish a fixed historical clock. Resemblance and regular repetition are different questions.

Cue: switch to time on “put those dates back”; enable guide before “An eighty-year guide”. Give the changing layout a full silent second. Do not describe this small curated sample as a statistical refutation of all cycle theories.

## 06 · Lives getting longer

Picture: Lives getting longer. World, India and Japan series. Replay the chart; show the labels and years clearly. Briefly reveal the evidence note. Use light theme for this whole shot if rehearsal confirms a clean cut from the preceding scene.

Narration:

> Some patterns can be explored through recorded data. Follow life expectancy across the years. Progress is uneven. Different places move at different speeds, and missing observations remain gaps. Behind each line is a question about ordinary lives: how long people could expect to live, and what helped that change.

Cue: playback starts on “across the years”. Avoid narrating a specific dip unless visible and supported by the source. Do not imply annual observations exist for every country throughout the timeline.

## 07 · Leave a question open

Picture: return to the Atlas, hold on a connected map with a selected moment, then return to the hero for a quiet ending. No sales CTA. Small History Loom title and a two-second tail.

Narration:

> Each view offers a different way to look. A story. A shared pressure. A change across generations. Follow the connection that surprises you. Then look closer.

Cue: end on a calm, readable screen. Let the sound decay naturally.
